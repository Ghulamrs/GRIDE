# cpp11 1.25 ("C++03 with C++11 extensions") - a critical review against ISO/IEC 14882:2011

Reviewer: Fable 5.1 (author of cppparse). Date: 2026-10-21. Subject: `cpp11.exe` 1.25 built from
`SCRATCH\g4\C++Optimize` (banner "©2026 G. R. Akhtar - C++03 with C++11 extensions"), target x86_64-windows, assembled
by the toolchain's masm and link, run against the UCRT. References: cl 19.52 (Visual Studio 2026, `/std:c++14 /permissive-`,
Microsoft's headers) as the behavioural oracle, and clang++ 22.1.3 `-std=c++11 -pedantic-errors` reading cpp11's own
headers (`-nostdinc -nostdinc++ -isystem g4out\bin\include`) as the strict-C++11 oracle. Every claim below is backed by a
program under `REVIEW = SCRATCH\cpp11-review\`; the runner is `REVIEW\run.ps1`, results are in each `<folder>-out\results.txt`.

## 1. Verdict

**cpp11 1.25 is not a C++11 compiler, and its own banner is the honest claim.** Judged as a C++11 implementation it
fails on four fronts, in this order of harm:

1. **Silent wrong behaviour on core C++ (15 distinct miscompiles found).** The worst are lifetime bugs - a temporary bound
   to `T&&` or `auto&&` is destroyed at the end of the full-expression (the program then writes to a dead object), a
   temporary whose member is bound to `const T&` is *never* destroyed (its destructor never runs), and a static local whose
   initialiser throws leaves the function hanging forever on the next call. The implicit move assignment operator is never
   generated (copy assignment runs instead), a user-declared copy assignment does not suppress the implicit move
   constructor, `std::move(obj).member` and `f("literal")` pick the `const&` overload instead of `&&`, the preprocessor
   evaluates `#if -1 > 0u` as false, and non-dependent names in templates are bound at instantiation (documented, but a
   conforming program changes behaviour).
2. **Robustness.** The compiler dies with a stack overflow (exit 0xC00000FD, after a 5-40 s Windows Error Reporting
   stall) on 80 nested parentheses, a class template recursion of depth 40, 300 nested blocks, a 300-arm else-if chain,
   50 nested lambdas or a 3000-term expression. cl and clang either compile these or refuse them with a message. cpp11.exe
   is linked with the default 1 MB stack and recurses through the whole parser per nesting level.
3. **Conformance breadth.** Of 268 valid programs, 99 ran identically to cl, 134 were refused. About half of the refusals
   are the documented "not supported yet" limits (refused by name, honestly), but a comparable number are *bugs*:
   valid C++98/C++11 refused by a wrong rule - `const auto &x = constObj` ("a reference that can write cannot bind to a
   const"), `template <class T> f(T &)` called with a const argument, a nested class reading members of its enclosing
   class declared after it, overload rankings ([over.ics.rank] 4.3/4.4 and 3.3) reported as ambiguous, an array of
   pointers to member functions typed as `void *`, a UTF-8 BOM, `__func__`, `_Pragma`, and more (section 3).
4. **The library is a subset on purpose** (the project says so) and several of its differences are behavioural, not
   just missing names: `vector::at` aborts instead of throwing, `std::swap` copies, move-only types cannot live in
   `vector`, `map::value_type` is `pair<K, V>` rather than `pair<const K, V>`.

What is good, and genuinely so: the Microsoft x64 calling convention and name decoration interoperate with cl objects
(structs of every size, non-trivial by-value parameters with callee destruction, varargs, virtual functions, RTTI and
exceptions of `int` and user classes thrown across cl/cpp11 frames in both directions, all identical to cl+cl); `-O2`
never changed a program's output in 268 programs; `enum class`, `nullptr`, `static_assert`, range-for, lambdas, variadic
type packs, constexpr functions and constructors, delegating and inheriting constructors, `override`/`final`,
list-initialisation of aggregates and `initializer_list` constructors all work when used plainly; the diagnostics prose
is the best of the three compilers; and the regression suite (706 cases) and the `EXCLUSIONS.md` discipline are real.

**Recommendation.** Keep the banner. Fix the lifetime, static-init and special-member miscompiles before anything else
(they turn correct programs into wrong ones without a diagnostic), raise the stack and bound the recursion, then work
through the wrongly-refused list - each of those is a common idiom. A C++11 claim needs two-phase lookup and a real
expression model for temporaries, which the parse-and-lower architecture (section 6) makes expensive.

| cpp11's own suite (session, 1.25) | 706 | 427 ran, 231 refused as expected, 28 skipped, 17 flagged (15 of them stale expectations) |

Verdicts over the 268 positive programs: **same as cl 99, differs 24, refused 134, compiler crash 1, link failure 2,
assembler failure 1, hang at run time 2, cl itself rejected 4** (cpp11 ran those; checked against clang). -O0 and -O2
agreed on every program (the two apparent disagreements are runner artefacts, noted in the results). Of the 24 "differs":
15 are cpp11 defects (listed in section 3), 3 are the library behaving differently, 3 are legal variance (execution
character set, copy elision), 2 are the documented two-phase decision, 1 is a test of mine with unspecified evaluation
order. Over the 80 ill-formed programs cpp11 refused 74 and **accepted 6** (clang refused all 80; cl 67).

Timing: cpp11's median compile was 35 ms (-O0) / 39 ms (-O2) against cl's 326 ms and clang's 76 ms syntax-only - it is a
fast compiler on small inputs; a million-statement file took 21.5 s (cl 4.2 s, clang 3.7 s).

## 4. Diagnostics and robustness

**Diagnostics (t19-diag, 20 programs beside clang and cl).** Position: `file:line:col` with a caret line, accurate on
every probe, including inside headers (d02), after raw strings (d06) and on tab-indented lines (d05). Wording: long,
explanatory, often citing the clause, listing candidates for ambiguous and non-matching calls (d11, d16) - the most
readable of the three. Refusals name the limit and usually the alternative ("the prefix form works", "write 'struct Ex
e(...)'"). Weaknesses: (1) **one error per translation unit** - compilation stops at the first (`Source::fail` throws
`CompileFailed`), where clang and cl report all three of d01; (2) **no warnings at all** (d07: unused variables,
`int n = 2.7`, `char c = 300`, `if (x = 3)` all silent - cl and clang warn); (3) **no instantiation context** - d04 and
d20 point into the template body with no "in instantiation of `L1<double>::go` requested here" chain, which makes
template errors hard to place; (4) the caret line is the **preprocessed** line, so a column inside a macro use points
into the expansion (d03 shows `((v) + ( undefined_name))`, not the source); (5) a few misleading messages: a missing `;`
after a class body gives "expected a name" at the next line (d09), the enum-operator case says the enum "does not
convert to int" (fo01), the nested-class cases say "has no member" (fo30, th03), `static virtual` says "'virtual' is
implemented, but not where a type was wanted" (neg64); (6) `const int &g() { return 5; }` is refused as an error
("returns a reference to a temporary") where it is well-formed - clang warns, cl says nothing (d14).

**Robustness (t16-robust: 48 generated inputs; t17-isolate: the limits by bisection).** No hangs. Malformed input is
handled well: unterminated strings/comments/`#if`, random ASCII, binary garbage ("this byte does not begin a UTF-8
character"), an empty file, unbalanced braces, a self-including file ("more than 32 deep"), a 1000-digit literal, an
infinite constexpr recursion ("more than 256 calls deep"), 10 000 functions, 5000 members, 10 000 cases, 50 000 string
literals, a 1 MB string, a 1 MB line and a 200 000-element initialiser all compile or are refused cleanly and quickly.
**Twelve inputs crash the compiler with a stack overflow** (exit -1073741571 = 0xC00000FD), each after a 5-40 s stall
while Windows Error Reporting runs:

| Construct | cpp11 crashes at | cl / clang |
|---|---|---|
| nested parentheses `((((1))))` | **80** (60 ok) | both fine at 1000; clang at 20 000 |
| class template recursion `Deep<N>` | **40** (30 ok) | fine at 500; refuse 5000 with a message |
| nested blocks `{ { { } } }` | **300** (200 ok) | clang fine at 2000 |
| `else if` chain | **300** (200 ok) | fine at 5000 (cl refuses) |
| nested lambdas | **50** (40 ok) | clang fine at 200 |
| left-associative chain `1 + 1 + ... ` | **3000 terms** (2000 ok) | cl fine at 50 000 |
| nested template arguments `A<A<A<...>>>` | 500 | both refuse with a limit message |
| `int (((((x)))))` declarator parentheses | 2000 | clang fine |
| an infinitely recursive function template `f(&t)` | crashes after 43 s | both refuse ("recursive template instantiation exceeded maximum depth") |
| a 2^20-token macro expansion | crashes (cl too, clang too) | - |

`cpp11.exe` is linked with a 1 MB stack reserve (PE header: SizeOfStackReserve 0x100000). The operator ladder is
iterative for left-associative chains (`ParserOperator.cpp`), so the 3000-term limit comes from a recursive walk over
the left-deep tree further down (folding or code generation); the paren limit (13 KB of stack per level) comes from the
full precedence ladder plus the type-id trial being re-entered per level; the template limit from re-entering the whole
parser per instantiation (token replay). Also: a UTF-8 BOM is a stray character (r22, th10), and a NUL byte is refused
where cl and clang ignore it (r37) - acceptable. Compile speed scales linearly where it does not crash (1 M statements
21.5 s, 20 000 locals 2.2 s, a 500-dimension array 1.8 s).
## 5. Code generation and ABI (x86_64-windows), and the other backends

**-O0 vs -O2.** All 268 programs gave the same output at both levels, including the deliberate optimiser traps in
`t13-opt` (aliasing through `char*` and through a pointer to a loop bound, globals modified by calls in loops,
division/modulo by constants with negative dividends at 32 and 64 bits, dense/sparse/64-bit/char switches,
struct copies of 1-32 bytes, inlined side effects and statics, floating-point strictness, bit-field RMW, dead-code with
side effects, 64-bit arithmetic, deep recursion with destructors). -O0 code is a stack machine (`push rax ... pop r10;
idiv r10d` for `x / 7`); -O2 turns it into `movsxd rax, ecx; mov r10d, 2454267027; imul; sar 34; ...` - the right magic
number - with locals in registers, loop rotation and `ALIGN 16`, a 3:1 reduction in lines (op02: 1264 -> 436). Run times
of the heavier tests are within 15% of cl /O2 (op12 285 ms vs 246; ex12 84 vs 94; op11 4.9 s vs 5.1 s). The optimiser is
sound on everything tried; its only observable deviation is the `volatile` downgrade (documented, warned).

**Calling convention and interop with cl objects (t15-abi, 12 pairs, both directions and cpp11+cpp11, each against
cl+cl).** Identical to cl+cl: structs of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 12, 16, 17, 24, 32 bytes and float/double/mixed structs
passed and returned both ways (abi01); scalar lists beyond four registers, mixed int/float positions, bool/char/short,
references, varargs with promotions (abi02); classes with non-trivial copy constructors and destructors passed by value
across the boundary - the Microsoft callee-destroys convention is honoured, live counts end at zero (abi06); virtual
functions declared in a cl TU and overridden in a cpp11 TU and vice versa, static members, overloads, namespaces,
operators, templates instantiated in both (abi03 BA); `typeid` equality and `dynamic_cast` on objects made by the other
compiler, `type_info` objects folded to one copy, globals with dynamic initialisation, `extern "C"`, class-template
statics shared (abi05); `wchar_t`/`char16_t` strings, callbacks both ways, a float pair through a function pointer
(abi08); exceptions of `int` and of user class hierarchies thrown in one compiler's code and caught in the other's,
unwinding through the other's frames with destructors, in both directions (abi09, abi10, abi11). Decorated names were
compared with `dumpbin` on ov12: constructors, constructor templates, member templates, overloads - byte for byte cl's.

Not interoperable: (1) **a static local of an inline function is one object per translation unit** - `inline int
counter() { static int n; ... }` called from two TUs counts twice, even when both TUs are cpp11's (abi05, abi08, all
three configurations differ from cl+cl) - an ODR violation at link time (the project documents the naming, not the
consequence); (2) `std::runtime_error` and friends cannot cross a cl/cpp11 boundary (abi04, abi12: heap corruption
0xC0000374 or an unhandled 0xE06D7363) because the two libraries' classes differ in layout while their `type_info`
names match - expected for a separate library, but it means no exception of a standard type may pass between the two;
(3) any class with two polymorphic bases or a virtual base with virtual functions is refused, so interface-style
hierarchies cannot be compiled at all on this target (abi07, cls04); (4) `void f(char * const)` decorates differently
(documented).

**The other backends (source reading only; not run).** `backend/X86_64Linux.cpp` (2509 lines) is the one x86-64 walker:
the Windows backend (`X86_64Windows.cpp`, 76 lines) only supplies the Microsoft `Abi` table (RCX/RDX/R8/R9, positional
classes, 32-byte shadow space, 8-byte struct limit, aggregates by reference, the wider callee-saved set) and chooses the
spelling - GNU syntax for clang's assembler, or `Masm.cpp`'s MASM spelling with COMDAT segments, SEH unwind codes,
`__CxxFrameHandler3` FuncInfo tables and funclets. The exception model follows the target (`usesFunclets()` =
Microsoft names), the Itanium model (LSDA call-site tables, `__cxa_*`) the ELF and Mach-O spellings; that is a clean
split and the reason both ABIs could be measured against the oracles. `Arm64Darwin.cpp` (1328 lines) is a second walker
of the same shape (AAPCS64 with Apple's variadic rule, `_tlv_atexit`, an `A64Peep` peephole pass instead of the x86
optimiser). `Tms6747.cpp` (2455 lines) plus `optimizer/C6xPipe.cpp` and `C6xSched.cpp` (4300 lines) is the most
ambitious piece: a C6000 walker "as a stack machine over A4, delay slots as NOPs" with a list scheduler and software
pipeliner for the VLIW, compact 16-bit instructions, TI's `rts6740` EH conventions measured on the simulator. The
Target interface (`sizeOf/alignOf/sizeType/wcharType/microsoftNames/...`) keeps the parser target-neutral, but the
parser still lays out frames and classes itself, so a new target is a new walker (~2500 lines) rather than a new
instruction selector over a shared IR.
## 6. Design review

**Architecture as read.** `Preprocessor` (1364 lines) works on *text lines*: macros are body strings, arguments are
collected as strings, `#if` is evaluated by `evalCondition` on `long long`; the output is one preprocessed text plus a
line map (`Source::Line`). `Lexer` tokenises the whole text into a `std::vector<Token>` (a fat token: three
`std::string`s, literal values and flags). `Parser` (26 000 lines in 15 files; `Parser.h` is one 2766-line class) is a
recursive-descent parser in which **parsing, semantic analysis and lowering are one pass**: it produces `Ast.h`'s tree,
which is not a C++ syntax tree but a C-level program - `Var` with frame offsets, `MemberAccess` with byte offsets,
`Call` with mangled symbols and result slots, references lowered to pointers ("the parser lowers it away, so no backend
sees this kind"), `volatile` dropped, class layout, vtables (`ParserVtable.cpp`), RTTI records and the Microsoft or
Itanium exception tables all decided in the parser, which is handed the `Abi` table to do it. Templates are
`TemplateDecl`s holding token ranges; **instantiation is token replay** - re-reading the declaration from
`afterParams` with the parameters bound in the name tables - to a fixed point (`instantiatePending`), with partial
specialisations matched as patterns, SFINAE as a `Trial` that turns `fail()` into `SubstitutionFailure`, and no
dependent AST, hence no two-phase lookup. Errors: `Source::fail` prints one diagnostic and throws `CompileFailed`; the
driver catches it per job and removes the output. Code generation: a `Walker` visitor per target emits instructions
through a `Spelling` interface; `Optimizer` is a `Spelling` *decorator* that holds the emitted x86 stream as
`opt::Function` (mnemonic plus operands), runs a pass pipeline (forward-values, remove-dead, coalesce copies, fold
loads/offsets/index, thread-jumps, divide-by-constant, webs -> pseudo registers, local promotion, a register allocator,
loop-invariant hoisting, counter widening, dead-store removal, loop alignment) and re-spells the result; inlining is the
walker walking the callee in place of the call, decided by an `Inliner` with GCC-style badness budgets. Manglers for both
ABIs live in `Mangle.cpp` (2061 lines). Test discipline: 706 cases with expected outputs, `.error` files for refusals,
`.notarget`, `tools/exclusions --check` tying every refusal to `EXCLUSIONS.md`.

**Strengths.** Self-contained, fast, four real targets with measured ABIs; the Windows calling convention, decoration,
RTTI and exception interop with cl are the strongest results of this review and are rare in a one-author compiler; the
optimiser is sound and gives cl-class code on the probes; the refuse-by-name rule means an unsupported feature is almost
never a silent failure (the exceptions are in section 3); the diagnostics prose and the documentation (`CONFORMANCE.md`,
`EXCLUSIONS.md`, per-decision notes in the source) are exemplary; the C6000 scheduler is serious compiler work.

**Structural risks, each tied to defects found here.**

1. *Parse-and-lower with no syntax tree.* Every semantic rule must be applied at the moment the tokens go by, with the
   frame already being laid out. This is why temporaries' lifetimes go wrong (a temporary is a slot whose destruction the
   parser schedules as it reads; the `T&&` binding, the member-of-temporary binding and the reference-returning call each
   need a different schedule and only `const T&` got one), why a nested class cannot see later members (its bodies are
   parsed when met, not deferred to the end of the enclosing class), why the implicit move assignment never appeared
   (the implicit-member table is built as the class closes, from flags), and why overload ranking has holes (ranking is
   done on lowered expressions whose value category is a pair of flags, `isXvalue`/`declCategory`, rather than a
   category of the expression tree - `std::move(s).s` loses it). Each fix is local, but each new rule lands in a
   26 000-line parser whose header already declares several hundred members.
2. *Token replay for templates.* It re-runs the whole parser per instantiation, which costs the stack (depth 40), the
   time (deep recursion crashed after 5-43 s of work), the instantiation chain in diagnostics (nothing remembers why an
   instantiation was requested), and two-phase lookup (names are looked up when replayed, so later declarations are
   found - fo34). The project calls this "the decision to revisit first if templates ever feel wrong"; they do.
3. *A line/string preprocessor.* Comments survive into macro arguments, an object-like macro expanding to a
   function-like macro name is not rescanned against the source's `(`, `#if` has no unsigned arithmetic, the BOM is a
   stray byte, and diagnostics inside macro uses point into the expansion. A token-based phase 3/4 fixes all five.
4. *One error then abort, and no warnings.* Cheap to live with in a suite that checks `.error` files, expensive for a
   user with a 20-error file; the `CompileFailed` design also forbids recovery, so every "expected X" ends the run.
5. *Recursion on a 1 MB stack with large frames.* Every nesting dimension overflows at depths real code reaches (an
   80-deep parenthesis is unusual; a 40-deep template or a 300-arm `else if` chain in generated code is not).
6. *An optimiser on textual assembly.* It must re-derive the semantics of each mnemonic (`OptEffects`), is x86-only, and
   cannot see what the walker knew (types, aliasing); it is sound today because the walker's output is regular, and it
   will stay sound only while every new instruction the walker emits is added to the effect tables.

**cppparse beside it.** cppparse (my own, 30 000 lines, strict C++98, Windows x64 only) keeps the four stages apart: a
token-based preprocessor (`Pp`), a syntax tree "exactly as written" (`CxxAst`), a separate `Sema` that interns types,
owns scopes and symbols, resolves overloads, lays out classes and instantiates templates *on the tree*, reporting coded
diagnostics and carrying on; `IrGen` lowers the annotated tree to a three-address IR with basic blocks, on which `Ssa`
and `Optimizer` work; `Lower`/`X86` select instructions, `MangleSema` decorates MSVC-style, `Coff` writes the object
without an assembler, `MapFile` the map; and an `analyzer` layer (CodeBase, Model, reports, health checks) that exists
because the tree exists. What cpp11 does better: it compiles C++11 - lambdas, rvalue references, variadic packs,
`constexpr`, range-for, `enum class`, `static_assert`, delegating/inheriting constructors - where cppparse stops at
C++98; it has four targets and both ABIs' exception models and manglers, measured; its register allocator, loop passes
and magic division give better x86 code than cppparse's store-forwarding IR optimiser and simple selector; its
calling-convention interop with cl has been proven here, where cppparse's own 2026-10-08 review found narrow-arithmetic
re-extension, missing stack probes and no EBO; and its diagnostics read better than cppparse's coded one-liners. What
cppparse does better: the syntax tree and the separate Sema make two-phase lookup, deferred member bodies, full-expression
temporary lists and multiple diagnostics natural (cppparse's Sema carries a dependent type kind and instantiates from the
tree); the token preprocessor has none of the four preprocessor defects above; the IR gives a target-neutral optimiser
and a place for the analyses a tool needs; the COFF writer removes the assembler from the loop; and the health checker
and reports are a different product altogether. What each could borrow: cpp11 should take a syntax tree with a separate
semantic pass (even keeping its lowered `Ast.h` as the *output* of that pass), a token preprocessor, an instantiation
stack for diagnostics, and a deeper stack; cppparse should take cpp11's measurement discipline (every layout and name
checked against clang and cl, the refuse-by-name rule, `tools/exclusions --check`), its register allocation and loop
passes, its exception tables for the Microsoft ABI (cppparse's runtime is its own and process-wide), and the habit of
explaining a refusal in a sentence a user can act on.
